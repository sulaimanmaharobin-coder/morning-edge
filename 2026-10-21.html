Top story

Claude designs toxin-binding proteins; 17 of 211 tested designs bind

Anthropic says Claude ran a 64-hour protein-design campaign against scorpion neurotoxins and released all the data.

Summary by AI. Source: Anthropic, via Hacker News · Tue 20 Oct 2026 · Read the original ↗

Anthropic published a research write-up on Oct 20 describing how Claude designed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins from four regions: Mexico (Cn2), Brazil (Ts1), North Africa (AaH I and II) and the Middle East (LqhIII). Anthropic says scorpion stings affect about 1.2 million people a year and cause more than 3,250 deaths.

According to the post, Claude generated 318,484 designs, and 211 were tested in the lab. Seventeen met the binding criteria, an 8% hit rate: seven against Cn2, four each against AaH I and AaH II, and two against LqhIII. The two best Cn2 binders (Cn2-B1 and Cn2-B2) showed single-digit nanomolar affinity. The run used roughly 10,000 graphics processing unit (GPU) hours and took 64 hours from first prompt to final results.

Anthropic lists clear limits. Testing used recombinant toxins only, with no cell or animal efficacy tests. No design bound multiple toxins at once, and Anthropic does not claim an antivenom. All 211 designs and the experimental data are public.

Why it matters to youYou can read it as a worked example of a Claude-driven generate, filter and test loop with an honest 8% hit rate, the same shape as a systematic strategy search you would backtest.
