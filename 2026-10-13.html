YC-backed Agent37 launches sandboxes for agents at under $5 a month

The Fall 2026 startup says it runs agent harnesses, including Claude Code, at a fraction of typical sandbox prices.

From Hacker News (Launch HN) · 2026-10-13

Agent37, a Y Combinator Fall 2026 company, launched on Hacker News with low-cost cloud sandboxes built for running AI agents. The founder, Vishnu Kool, says the service costs under 10% of most sandbox providers.

Paid plans start at about $4.94 a month for a 2 vCPU, 4 GB configuration. The company says the average paid customer spends about $0.37 a month per sandbox. A free tier is available, which the company says costs it roughly $0.05 to $0.10 in infrastructure per user each month. It claims to be 2.8 times cheaper than Boat.dev and 94% cheaper than Fly.io Sprites. Those figures come from the company, not from independent tests.

The platform offers both gVisor-based containers and full VM sandboxes. It supports several agent harnesses, naming Hermes, OpenClaw and Claude Code. It also provides OpenAI-compatible API endpoints and managed integrations including Composio and BraveSearch. The launch thread had 77 points when checked and was ranked #10 on the front page.

Why it matters to youIf you run Claude Code teams unattended overnight, an isolated, cheap sandbox is a way to cap blast radius and cost, though the pricing claims need testing against your own workloads.

Bitcoin and ether order books rebuilt a year after 10/10 crash

Bitcoin depth within 1% of price reached $11.7 million on Oct 7, 75% deeper than on the crash day. ETH depth within 1% rose 75% to $5.3 million, but altcoin depth at 5% fell about a third to roughly $2 million.

Bitcoin logs 10 three-sigma days in 2026 despite falling volatility

Annualized volatility fell to 46% from 84%, yet 2026 already has 10 three-sigma days against eight in all of 2018. Analysts warn that value-at-risk models built on recent volatility understate tail risk.

Why it matters If your backtests size positions from trailing volatility, stress-test them against fat-tail days.

Python 3.15 arrives; Simon Willison drops 3.10 support

3.15 adds explicit lazy imports, unpacking in comprehensions and a built-in sampling profiler called Tachyon. Willison says dropping 3.10 lets his projects use 3.11 features such as asyncio TaskGroup and tomllib. He found performance gains modest outside the experimental JIT.

Why it matters Tachyon could help you profile slow loops when porting Pine Script indicators to local Python backtests.
