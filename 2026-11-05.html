Claude Marketplace adds 13 partner agents, now over 25 in total

Anthropic says enterprise customers can pay for partner tools out of their committed Claude spend.

Claude blog (Anthropic) · Wed 4 Nov 2026 · Read the original ↗

Anthropic on November 4 added 13 agents and products to Claude Marketplace, which now lists more than 25 partner offerings, according to the company's announcement.

Five are in security: Chainguard, Checkmarx, Cyera, Rubrik and Tanium, covering vulnerability scanning, code analysis and endpoint management. Two are developer infrastructure: Datadog for agent observability and Netlify for production deployment, which Anthropic describes as needing no infrastructure to configure. Three target creative and marketing work (AirOps, Runway, Webflow), and four target productivity (Asana, Glean, monday, Zoom).

The commercial change is that enterprise customers can apply committed Anthropic spend to these partner products, so purchasing runs through one contract. The announcement does not say how partner agents are priced, how they are sandboxed, or how they authenticate to a customer's systems; those details are not in the article.

For people who build agents on Claude, the Datadog entry is the one to watch: observability for unattended agent runs is a gap many builders fill with home-made logging.

If you run Claude agents unattended overnight, a vendor-supported observability layer like Datadog's could replace your own logging, though you'd need to check the pricing and access terms first.

Conductor, a multi-agent coding workspace, is joining Meta

The Conductor team says it is joining Meta. According to its announcement, new signups stop immediately, cloud workspaces go read-only on Nov 13, and cloud data deletion begins Nov 20. The local version keeps getting new models and critical bug fixes until early 2027. The page's own year references are inconsistent, so check the dates against your account.

If you use Conductor to run parallel Claude Code sessions, export any cloud workspaces before Nov 13.

Ripple-65B open-weight model claims top score on agentic coding benchmark

Ricursive Intelligence released a 65-billion-parameter open-weight model, trained from scratch for chip design, and reports 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1. It lists Reflection Beam 501B at 80.1 and Meta Muse Spark Open 816B at 76.4. Ricursive says it used 1.4 million reinforcement-learning rollouts and a $32 million total R&D budget; the scores are the company's own.

A small open model that scores near larger ones could serve as a cheap worker model in your overnight multi-agent setups, once independently tested.

Anthropic research: proving a model's answer came from the claimed model

Anthropic describes FlashProve, which uses zero-knowledge proofs to let users verify a response came from a specific model without access to its weights. Proof generation runs at 110 tokens per second on one H100 GPU, with about 19 MB per 1,024-token request, and is 70 to 95 times slower than normal inference. Anthropic notes it covers only integer-based greedy decoding and contexts up to 1,024 tokens.

It shows where verifiable agent outputs could go, which matters if you ever sell agent-generated research or signals and need to prove provenance.

Bitcoin rebounds to $78,300; futures volume up 27%, open interest only 3%

According to CoinDesk, bitcoin recovered from about $75,600 after midterm results to roughly $78,300. Market-wide futures volume rose 27% in 24 hours while open interest rose 3%, which CoinDesk reads as repositioning rather than new capital. Liquidations rose 22% to $469 million, options volatility fell from about 41% to 34%, and the fear and greed index was 37.

A sharp dip-and-reclaim on rising volume with flat open interest is the pattern your reclaim-with-volume perps entries look for, but confirm on your own chart rather than chasing.

A Read the Diff post, relayed on Hacker News, says Replit Pro subscribers must opt out by November 8 to keep the right to sue rather than go to arbitration. Only the post's headline was reviewed for this item, so check Replit's terms for the exact opt-out process.
