Anthropic sales rep runs five-agent crew that he says saves 60 hours a week

A business development representative in Japan describes a fleet of scheduled Claude Managed Agents handling leads, email, Salesforce updates and meeting follow-ups.

Claude (Anthropic) · Fri 16 Oct 2026 · Read the original ↗

Shahboz Ibragimov, a business development representative (BDR) at Anthropic Japan, published a write-up on October 16 of how he runs his day with Claude Managed Agents. He says the setup saves about 60 hours per week. The figure is his own estimate, not an independent measurement.

He calls the system "the Nakama" (Japanese for "crew"): five specialised agents plus a sixth coordinator. One sorts inbound leads and routes them to teammates. One qualifies leads and drafts first replies. One prepares hourly email responses in Japanese or English. One updates Salesforce records from customer interactions. One summarises meetings and drafts follow-ups. The coordinator manages instructions and a shared knowledge base.

The article lists the building blocks: scheduled deployments that run in the background, shared memory stores, managed vaults for credentials with scoped permissions, and a tool called Claude Tag that escalates complex decisions to a human in Slack. He uses Sonnet for rule-based tasks and Opus for judgment-heavy work, plus an "advisor" strategy to keep costs down.

The post is a first-party Anthropic case study, so it shows the design but gives no failure rates or error figures.

It is a concrete blueprint for the 24/7 outreach and unattended multi-agent setups you build: split roles, shared memory, scoped credentials, human escalation, and cheaper models for routine steps.
