Study: Agent swarms finish faster but score lower than one agent

Paradigm 3's 1,100-run test of Claude and GPT teams finds that a single agent restarted with fresh context matches or beats swarms at the same token budget.

Paradigm 3, via TLDR AI · Tue 24 Nov 2026 · Read the original ↗

Researchers Niccolò Zanichelli and Gavin Leech at Paradigm 3 ran more than 1,100 runs across 80-plus team configurations. They pitted teams of 2, 4 and 8 agents against a single agent with the same total token budget, mostly 8 million tokens and in some runs 32 million. The models were Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6.1 Terra. Tasks included reimplementing git in Zig, two AtCoder Heuristic Contest problems and Anthropic's performance take-home.

According to the authors, teams of 8 finished roughly six times sooner but typically scored about 7% lower. On the git-to-Zig task at 32 million tokens, Sol teams of 8 scored 93.2% against 83.7% for a single agent. A single agent restarted across eight fresh sessions, keeping its files on disk, scored 97.0%. Teams of 32 scored 23.2%.

The authors conclude that the apparent 'swarm premium' comes mostly from single agents degrading over long sessions, and that swarms buy speed rather than quality. They caveat that budgets were small compared with lab-scale runs, that most configurations ran only three times, that the restarted baseline was tested only on git-to-Zig, and that results may not hold for future models. They put the cost of replicating the experiments at about $70,000 at API prices.

Before you add more agents to your overnight Claude team, benchmark it against one agent restarted in fresh sessions that share files, at the same token spend.

Derive lists onchain options on Nvidia, Anthropic and oil

Derive.xyz launched cash-settled options on Nvidia (NVDA), Anthropic (ANT), equity index futures and West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude. The options settle in USDC, have daily, weekly and monthly expiries, trade 24/7, and accept crypto such as bitcoin as collateral. 1Password says attacker pushed malicious code to its backend

1Password says an attacker used an employee's corporate GitHub account to reach source code and its continuous integration and delivery (CI/CD) system, then deployed code that downloaded administrative data for a subset of users. The company says vault contents, Secret Keys and the apps were not affected, and it has rotated its CI/CD secrets. It has not said how many users were affected.

If your agents pull secrets from 1Password, expect phishing that mentions recovery or invitation links, and enter credentials only in the official app.

Cognition's SWE-2.1 roughly doubles coding benchmark scores, trails Opus

Cognition says SWE-2.1, trained with reinforcement learning on Kimi K3, scores 50.0 on Terminal-Bench 4.1, up from 24.2 for SWE-2. By Cognition's own figures it still trails Claude Opus 5.5 (65.8 on Terminal-Bench, 73.1 vs 54.9 on SWE Marathon). Cognition says it is free on Devin Desktop and the command-line interface (CLI) until December 8. Scores are self-reported.

It is a free option for cheap sub-agent coding work until December 8, but on Cognition's own numbers Opus 5.5 should stay your lead model.

Ten accounts drove all XRP Ledger order-book growth

CoinDesk, using data from XRP holder Evernorth, reports that average daily order-book volume rose 26% to 4.51 million XRP in the third quarter. The 10 busiest accounts took 65% of volume, up from 48%, while volume from all other accounts fell. CoinDesk notes that the data was not independently verified and that more volume does not mean deeper liquidity.

It is a reminder to filter your volume-confirmation rule by number of participants, not raw volume, on thin altcoin books.
